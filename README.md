# Proposal: AI Content Disclosure in the Open RSS Feed

## Summary

Adopt a single, interoperable convention for episode-level AI content disclosure in the open RSS feed, using the existing `podcast:txt` tag with **three states: yes, no, and not-answered.** One convention that every podcast hosting company writes the same way and every app can read the same way.

This is an **adoption** proposal, not a new-tag proposal. The tag already exists in the Podcasting 2.0 namespace and some podcast hosting companies already ship a version of this today. The PSP's job here is to standardize the exact form so the signal is portable and machine-readable across the whole ecosystem.

Want to see it in action first? [Play with the interactive demo](https://albertobeta.github.io/ai-disclosure-rss-proposal/).

## Problem

AI-generated audio is now a large and growing share of new podcasts. Listeners increasingly want to know whether what they are hearing was performed by a human or generated by a machine. Platforms and regulators are moving in the same direction: Apple Podcasts already requires disclosure when AI generates "a material portion of the podcast's audio," and the EU AI Act (Article 50) takes effect in August 2026.

The problem is that without an agreed convention, the same intent can be expressed by different podcast hosting companies in incompatible ways. Two hosts can both believe they "support AI disclosure" while writing different tags, different attribute strings, or different value semantics. When that happens, no app can reliably read the signal, and the disclosure data, though present, is effectively invisible to listeners.

Who experiences this:
- **Listeners** cannot trust or even see a disclosure signal, because apps cannot reliably parse a non-standard field.
- **Creators** who do the right thing and disclose get no portable benefit, because the signal does not travel.
- **Apps** have no single field to filter, badge, or surface.
- **Hosts** each reinvent the convention, and the fragmentation defeats the purpose.

## Value hypothesis

- **For listeners:** a trustworthy, portable signal about AI content that works the same way in every app, not just inside one closed platform.
- **For creators:** one transparency action, taken once at publish time, that travels across every app that reads RSS and future-proofs them against incoming regulation (EU AI Act, August 2026).
- **For apps:** a single, predictable field they can read to render a badge, offer a filter, or inform recommendations.
- **For the open ecosystem:** keeps AI disclosure a property of the open feed (portable, verifiable, platform-agnostic, persistent) rather than something locked inside Apple, Spotify, or any single platform's private metadata.

## Evidence of demand

**Platforms already require or are building it:**
- **Apple Podcasts** (Content Guidelines 1.11, 1.12): disclosure required when AI generates a material portion of the audio.
- **Spotify** (September 2025): three-tier human / AI-assisted / fully-AI model; prohibits unconsented voice cloning.
- **YouTube:** requires disclosure of realistic synthetic content (exempts scriptwriting, ideation, cleanup).
- **EU AI Act** (Article 50, effective August 2026): deepfakes and AI text on matters of public interest must be disclosed; fines up to 15M EUR or 3% of global turnover.

**The community is already discussing it:**
- Several Podcasting 2.0 namespace discussions on Github spanning over one year.
- Covered repeatedly on Podnews Weekly Review.
- Raised at the PSP London meetup (May 2026) as [one of four priority proposals](https://podstandards.org/2026/05/22/podcast-rss-nerds-unite-in-london/).

**Some creators are already doing a manual version of this** in show notes ("this episode used an AI voice"), which is exactly the "people are already doing some version of this manually" signal the PSP looks for.

## Proposed solution

### The three-state model

The core of the proposal is that disclosure has **three** states, not two. The third state, "not answered," is what makes the data honest.

| State | Meaning | Written to RSS feed (`<item>` level)|
|-------|---------|---------------------|
| **Yes** | The creator confirms the episode includes AI-generated content (per [the substance rule below](#shared-editorial-guidance-the-substance-test)). | `<podcast:txt purpose="ai-content">true</podcast:txt>` |
| **No** | The creator confirms the episode does NOT include AI-generated content. | `<podcast:txt purpose="ai-content">false</podcast:txt>` |
| **Not answered** | The creator has not engaged with the question, or the episode predates the feature. | No tag written. |

### Why three states, not a two-state checkbox

This is the heart of the proposal. A simple yes/no checkbox cannot distinguish an active "no" from silence, and that distinction is not a nicety. It is what keeps the data honest.

**1. Retro-compatibility: we must never infer "false" from absence.** AI disclosure is a brand-new property. Tens of millions of episodes already exist in the open ecosystem, published before this question existed, and not one of them was ever asked. If we treat "no tag" as "false," we instantly stamp every one of those episodes with a claim its creator never made: "this episode contains no AI content." At the scale of the open feed that is not a rounding error but mass-fabricated metadata, and it would actively mislead the listeners and regulators the disclosure is meant to serve. The only truthful reading of a missing tag is **unknown**, and the only way to preserve "unknown" is a state that writes nothing. A two-state checkbox has no way to represent "unknown," so it cannot be retro-compatible. This point alone is decisive.

**2. A non-answer is itself an answer, and a different one from "no."** A creator who actively declares "no, this is not AI" is making a statement and standing behind it. A creator who leaves the question unanswered has made a different choice: they have chosen not to disclose. Those are not the same thing, and apps, directories, and eventually regulators may reasonably treat them differently. Collapsing silence and "no" into one bucket throws away real information. (This concern was raised by John Spurlock in the PSP Slack; a two-state model loses it.)

**3. It never forces a false choice at publish time.** With only yes/no, a creator who has not considered the question, or who simply does not wish to declare, is forced to assert something anyway. Three states let silence stay silent.

### Why a binary value, not a graduated scale

The value is `true` / `false`, not a percentage or a tier. Reasons: it mirrors `itunes:explicit`; graduated systems create decision fatigue ("is research-with-ChatGPT 'AI-assisted'?"); and machines need a boolean they can filter on, not a "12% AI" field that means nothing to an algorithm. A graduated scale would also introduce measurement bias. Different hosts would collect it through different user interfaces, and the design of the input control affects the answer, so the numbers would not be comparable across the ecosystem. A horizontal slider, for example, can produce systematically different results from a vertical one, or from a five-point radio group. Nuance belongs in show notes, not in the tag.

### Channel-level disclosure: inferred from items, never authored

Disclosure is authored at the **item (episode) level**, because AI use is an episode-by-episode decision. But apps and directories may want a single answer for the whole show. We propose deriving a **channel-level** value from the items, conservatively, rather than asking creators to maintain a separate show-level flag that could drift out of sync.

The same three-state honesty applies (`true` / `false` / absent), and the governing principle is simple: **the channel value must never claim more than the items collectively prove.** That makes the rule deliberately asymmetric. A single `true` item is enough to make the channel `true`, but the channel may only be `false` when **every** item is explicitly `false`. One AI episode means the show contains AI; one undeclared episode means we cannot yet certify the whole show as AI-free.

| What the items declare | Channel-level `podcast:txt purpose="ai-content"` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One or more items declare `true` | **`true`** | One is enough. If any episode contains AI content, the show demonstrably contains AI content. Safe regardless of the other items. |
| All items explicitly declare `false`: none `true`, none un-answered (an empty feed does not qualify and falls to Absent below) | **`false`** | Every single episode has been individually declared AI-free, so the whole show is AI-free. The claim is fully backed by the items. |
| Anything else: any un-answered item (or a feed with no items), and no `true` | **Absent** (write nothing) | At least one episode is unknown, so we can neither claim the show contains AI nor that it is AI-free. Unknown stays unknown. |

In short: the channel mirrors the item-level three states, but it earns each one strictly. It says `true` the moment any episode is `true`, it says `false` only when every episode has explicitly said `false`, and it stays silent whenever a single episode is left undeclared. A `true` needs one witness; a `false` needs unanimity; anything short of unanimity is unknown. This is the item-level "not answered" principle applied one level up: we never assert what the items do not collectively prove.

### Example feed output

```xml
<channel>
  <title>The Future of Audio</title>

  <!-- Channel-level: inferred true because at least one item below is true. -->
  <podcast:txt purpose="ai-content">true</podcast:txt>

  <item>
    <title>Episode 42: AI-narrated special</title>
    <guid isPermaLink="false">abc-123</guid>
    <enclosure url="https://example.com/ep42.mp3" type="audio/mpeg" length="29000000"/>
    <!-- Creator answered YES -->
    <podcast:txt purpose="ai-content">true</podcast:txt>
  </item>

  <item>
    <title>Episode 41: Two human hosts</title>
    <guid isPermaLink="false">abc-122</guid>
    <enclosure url="https://example.com/ep41.mp3" type="audio/mpeg" length="31000000"/>
    <!-- Creator answered NO -->
    <podcast:txt purpose="ai-content">false</podcast:txt>
  </item>

  <item>
    <title>Episode 40: published before the feature existed</title>
    <guid isPermaLink="false">abc-121</guid>
    <enclosure url="https://example.com/ep40.mp3" type="audio/mpeg" length="28000000"/>
    <!-- Not answered: no podcast:txt element at all -->
  </item>
</channel>
```

For an item, "no" writes `false` and "not answered" writes nothing. At the channel level the value is `true` here only because Episode 42 is `true`. Remove Episode 42 and the channel element becomes **absent**, because the not-answered Episode 40 means the show can no longer be certified AI-free. Mark Episode 40 explicitly `false` as well (so every item is `false`) and the channel reads **`false`**.

### Shared editorial guidance: The Substance Test

A convention only works if creators answer it consistently, so standardizing what `true` and `false` *mean* matters as much as standardizing the tag itself. There is already a concrete, actionable, plain-language guide the PSP can point podcasters to: **The Substance Test**, at [shouldidisclose.ai](https://shouldidisclose.ai).

It reduces the decision to a single question:

> **Is AI doing the creative work my listeners came for?**

If AI is the **performer** (delivering the content as speech, narration, or conversation): disclose. If AI is a **production tool** (cleanup, music beds, jingles, show notes, transcripts, scriptwriting assistance): no disclosure needed. This mirrors Apple's "material portion" language and aligns with YouTube, the EU AI Act, and the Partnership on AI.

The full test, with worked examples and the hard edge cases (self-voice cloning, AI-read listener questions, translated episodes), lives at [shouldidisclose.ai](https://shouldidisclose.ai) as a free resource any host can link directly from its publishing flow.

### What the host UI looks like

The control lives where the podcaster already works: the page where they publish a new episode or edit an existing one. There is exactly one hard requirement: the UI must be able to represent **all three states**, with **"not answered" as the default** (no claim written unless the creator makes one).

**Standardize the wording, not just the tag.** The easiest path for everyone is for hosts to phrase the question the same way. If every platform asks it identically, creators learn one mental model regardless of which host they use, and any listener-facing label stays consistent across the ecosystem. We suggest hosts adopt this shared copy:

- **Question:** "Does this episode include AI content?"
- **Yes option:** "Yes" (writes `true`)
- **No option:** "No" (writes `false`)
- **Default:** neither selected (not answered)

**Pair it with a one-line help affordance.** The most common creator question is "does my use of AI even count?", so a small info icon or tooltip next to the question is worth including. It can answer inline and link out to fuller guidance. For example:

> Disclose when AI is the performer (synthetic voice, narration, hosts), not when it is just a production tool (editing, music, notes). **[Learn more]**

where "Learn more" points to the host's own help-center article, and, if a host wants to offer a concrete shared rule, to [The Substance Test at shouldidisclose.ai](https://shouldidisclose.ai).

**The widget itself is the host's choice.** The recommended copy works equally well as a dropdown with no default selection, as two radio options with neither preselected (the pattern RSS.com ships today), or as any pair of buttons or toggles, as long as "pick neither" stays valid. The standard constrains the three states and the default, not the control.

**This is never a mandatory field.** Leaving it unanswered is a first-class outcome, not an error to fix. We are not adding a gate to publishing, only the opportunity to disclose. A creator who would rather not engage with the question publishes exactly as before, and nothing is written to the feed.

On publish: write the tag for Yes or No, write nothing for unanswered, and never backfill historical episodes. A standalone interactive demo of this control accompanies this proposal. See `ai-disclosure-ui.html` in this repo or  [the live demo](https://albertobeta.github.io/ai-disclosure-rss-proposal/).

### How apps implement

Read `podcast:txt` elements, find the one with the agreed `purpose`, and read `true` / `false`. Absent element = unknown, render nothing (same as today). Apps may render a small badge, offer an AI-content filter, feed the signal into recommendations, or simply start collecting the signal for now and decide what to surface later.
